\chapter{Thermal noise and nucleation}
\label{ch:p8}

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{Key idea.} Where do the crystal seeds of Chapter~\ref{ch:p6} come
from? From thermal \emph{fluctuations}. An undercooled melt is
\emph{metastable}: $\psi=0$ sits behind a free-energy barrier, and the
crystal cannot form until a fluctuation pushes a small region over that
barrier --- classical nucleation. Here the noise is \emph{physics}, and
its amplitude ($k_BT$) sets the nucleation rate.
\end{keybox}

\section{The model}

We add the physical (fluctuation--dissipation) noise to the Allen--Cahn
crystallinity equation:
%
\begin{equation}
  \dd{\psi}{t} = -L_\psi\Big[\dd{f}{\psi}-\varepsilon^2\grad^2\psi\Big]
   + \xi,\qquad
  \langle\xi(\mathbf{x},t)\,\xi(\mathbf{x}',t')\rangle
   = 2L_\psi\,k_BT\,\delta(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}')\delta(t-t'),
\end{equation}
%
an FDT-normalized Gaussian forcing with $\texttt{noise\_psi}^2=k_BT$ in
the nondimensional units. The $p1$ bulk free energy has a genuine
nucleation barrier at $\psi=0$ ($\partial f/\partial\psi=\phi^2 N^{-1}
\Delta\sigma>0$ there), so with \emph{no} noise $\psi$ stays $0$ forever;
with noise, rare fluctuations climb the barrier, form supercritical
nuclei, and those grow.

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{The physical seed.} In Chapter~\ref{ch:p1} the random seed was a
\emph{numerical} convenience (a fixed RNG seed; the statistics were
seed-independent). \emph{Here} the noise is a first-class physical
object: its amplitude is $k_BT$ and it sets the nucleation \emph{rate}.
Nucleation is a rare-event, barrier-crossing process, so the rate scales
like $\exp(-\Delta F^\star/k_BT)$ --- a small increase in noise amplitude
produces a large increase in how much crystal forms by a fixed time.
(BDF1 only: the brick asserts noise off under BDF2, the stochastic weak
order being a recorded scope limit.)
\end{keybox}

\section{Run it}

\begin{runbox}
\textbf{Run it.}
\begin{lstlisting}
cd physics/08_noise_nucleation
python run.py            # sweep the FDT noise amplitude
\end{lstlisting}
It runs the same undercooled melt ($\psi$ starts at $0$, \emph{no} seed)
at several noise amplitudes and prints the final crystalline fraction,
the grain count, and the induction time. Compare with \file{EXPECTED.md}
and the figures.
\end{runbox}

\paragraph{What you should see.} With zero noise the melt stays amorphous
($X=\PeightXzero$: no nucleation). Increasing the FDT amplitude nucleates
crystal --- by the largest amplitude ($\texttt{noise\_psi}=\PeightNoiseHi$)
the melt reaches $X=\PeightXhi$ in $\PeightGrainsHi$ distinct grains. The
onset is threshold-like: nucleation switches on between the amplitudes,
reflecting the exponential barrier-crossing rate. The noise is the
physical seed.

\section{Code walk-through}

\paragraph{1. Turn on the FDT noise.} Two constructor arguments:
\begin{lstlisting}
st = MultiPhaseStepper(dm, M=1, K=1, ..., tstep="bdf1",
                       noise_psi=0.06, noise_seed=17)
\end{lstlisting}
\code{noise\_psi} is $\sqrt{k_BT}$ (the amplitude), \code{noise\_seed}
fixes the fluctuation realization for reproducibility.

\paragraph{2. No seed.} \code{set\_initial([phi], [zeros], [zeros])}
starts from a uniform amorphous melt --- the crystal must nucleate on its
own.

\paragraph{3. The sweep.} \code{sweep} runs several amplitudes on the
same melt and reads $X_{\text{end}}$, the grain count
(\code{grain\_labels}), and the induction time.

\question{The nucleation rate should scale like $\exp(-\Delta
F^\star/k_BT)$. Plot $\ln(\text{grains})$ (or $\ln X$) against
$1/\texttt{noise\_psi}^2$. Is it roughly linear, and what does the slope
estimate about the barrier height?}

\question{Deepen the undercooling (lower $T$) at fixed noise. Does the
induction time fall? Relate this to how the barrier $\Delta F^\star$
depends on the driving force.}

\question{Change only the \code{noise\_seed} at fixed amplitude. Which is
reproducible --- the exact grain \emph{positions}, or the statistics
(grain count, $X$)? Connect this to the seed discussion in
Chapter~\ref{ch:p1}: here the seed is physical, so a different seed is a
different (equally valid) sample of the same fluctuating system.}

\question{Is there an amplitude so small that nothing nucleates within
the run, yet the melt is still undercooled? What does that say about
metastability and the difference between \emph{unstable} (spinodal) and
\emph{metastable} (nucleation) states?}

\question{Combine with Chapter~\ref{ch:p6}: once a fluctuation nucleates a
supercritical crystal, does it grow at the same rate as a hand-placed
