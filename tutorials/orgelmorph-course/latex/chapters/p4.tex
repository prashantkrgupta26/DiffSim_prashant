\chapter{Ternary Cahn--Hilliard and the phase diagram}
\label{ch:p4}

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{Key idea.} A real cast blend has (at least) three components ---
two solutes and a solvent. With two independent conserved compositions
the free energy is a \emph{surface over the Gibbs triangle}, and demixing
follows \emph{tie-lines} on that triangle. We do not eyeball the tie-line:
we \emph{derive} the spinodal from the free-energy Hessian, \emph{predict}
the binodal by a common-tangent construction, and then \emph{measure} the
two coexisting phases from the simulation by clustering the composition
cloud --- theory and simulation compared on one triangle.
\end{keybox}

\section{The model, with the \texorpdfstring{$N_i$}{N} factors}

Let $\phi_1,\phi_2$ be the volume fractions of the two solutes; the
solvent is eliminated by $\phi_s = 1-\phi_1-\phi_2$. The Flory--Huggins
free-energy density (in $kT/v_0$ units) is
%
\begin{equation}
  f(\phi_1,\phi_2) = \sum_{i\in\{1,2,s\}} \frac{\phi_i}{N_i}\ln\phi_i
    \;+\; \sum_{i<j}\chi_{ij}\,\phi_i\phi_j ,
  \label{eq:p4f}
\end{equation}
%
where $N_i$ is the degree of polymerization of species $i$ (entropic:
a long chain has $N_i\gg1$ and a \emph{weak} mixing entropy $\propto1/N_i$)
and $\chi_{ij}$ the pairwise interaction (enthalpic). The exchange
chemical potentials, eliminating the solvent, are
%
\begin{equation}
  \mu_i = \pd{f}{\phi_i}
    = \frac{1}{N_i}\big(\ln\phi_i+1\big)
      - \frac{1}{N_s}\big(\ln\phi_s+1\big)
      + \chi_{12}\phi_j + \chi_{is}(\phi_s-\phi_i) - \chi_{js}\phi_j,
  \quad j\neq i,
  \label{eq:p4mu}
\end{equation}
%
and the coupled conserved dynamics is
%
\begin{equation}
  \dd{\phi_i}{t} = \grad\!\cdot\!\Big(\textstyle\sum_j M_{ij}\grad\mu_j\Big),
  \qquad \mu_i = \mu_i^{\text{bulk}} - \kappa_i\grad^2\phi_i ,
\end{equation}
%
with $M$ a symmetric positive-definite Onsager mobility (its off-diagonal
couples the two solute fluxes) and $\kappa_i$ the gradient penalties. This
is Chapter~\ref{ch:p1} promoted to two composition fields; in the code it
is the $(M{=}2,K{=}0)$ case of the production
\code{MultiPhaseStepper}, with the same \eqref{eq:p4f} bulk (\code{bulk="p1"}).

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{Coefficients come from a materials file, not from code.} The run
reads $(\chi_{12},\chi_{1s},\chi_{2s})$ and $(N_1,N_2,N_s)$ from
\file{materials/ternary\_p4.yaml} through the validated loader
\file{materials/loader.py} --- each parameter carries
\texttt{value/units/definition/source/uncertainty}, so no number is
hand-copied. The default \texttt{synthetic\_demix} blend is
$\chi_{12}=\PfourChiab$, $\chi_{1s}=\PfourChias$, $\chi_{2s}=\PfourChibs$,
$N=(\PfourNone,\PfourNtwo,\PfourNs)$ --- a deliberately strong quench so the
symmetric blend sits well inside the spinodal.
\end{keybox}

\section{The spinodal: where the Hessian loses definiteness}

A blend demixes spontaneously (spinodal decomposition) where $f$ is
locally concave in some direction --- i.e.\ where the $2\times2$ exchange
Hessian $H_{ij}=\partial^2 f/\partial\phi_i\partial\phi_j$ stops being
positive definite. Differentiating \eqref{eq:p4mu},
%
\begin{align}
  H_{11} &= \frac{1}{N_1\phi_1} + \frac{1}{N_s\phi_s} - 2\chi_{1s}, &
  H_{22} &= \frac{1}{N_2\phi_2} + \frac{1}{N_s\phi_s} - 2\chi_{2s}, \notag\\
  H_{12} &= \frac{1}{N_s\phi_s} + \chi_{12} - \chi_{1s} - \chi_{2s}.
  \label{eq:p4H}
\end{align}
%
The blend is unstable where $\det H = H_{11}H_{22}-H_{12}^2 < 0$; the
curve $\det H = 0$ is the \emph{spinodal}. At the initial blend
$(\PfourPhione,\PfourPhitwo)$ the code evaluates \eqref{eq:p4H} and finds
$\det H = \PfourDet$ (smaller eigenvalue $\lambda_{\min}=\PfourEigMin<0$):
the quench is unstable, as it must be for the noise to grow rather than
decay. Because $H_{12}$ is the only entry carrying $\chi_{12}$, we can
solve $\det H=0$ for the \emph{critical} interaction at this composition,
$\chi_{12}^\star=\sqrt{H_{11}H_{22}}-(1/N_s\phi_s-\chi_{1s}-\chi_{2s})
=\PfourChiCrit$; any $\chi_{12}>\chi_{12}^\star$ demixes.

\section{The binodal: a common-tangent prediction}

The spinodal bounds the \emph{unstable} region; the two compositions the
system actually separates \emph{into} lie on the \emph{binodal}, defined
by equal chemical potentials and equal osmotic (grand) potential in the
two phases, $\mu_i(\vec\phi^A)=\mu_i(\vec\phi^B)$ and
$\omega(\vec\phi^A)=\omega(\vec\phi^B)$ with
$\omega=f-\sum_i\mu_i\phi_i$ --- the common-tangent-plane construction.
Pinning the tie-line to pass through the conserved mean (the lever rule)
closes the system; the code solves it by a small bounded least-squares
step, giving the predicted endpoints $\PfourBinodalA$ and $\PfourBinodalB$
(in $(\phi_1,\phi_2)$). These are the theory the simulation is checked
against.

\section{Reading the two phases: cluster, don't median-split}

The naive tie-line readout --- split the final field at the median
$\phi_1$ and average each half --- is biased: it forces a $50/50$ split
and lets interfacial (mixing-transient) nodes contaminate both means.
Instead we treat the cloud of local compositions
$\{(\phi_1,\phi_2)\}_{\text{nodes}}$ as a two-population sample and fit a
\textbf{2-component Gaussian mixture} (EM; no \texttt{sklearn} needed).
Each phase gets a \emph{mean}, a $2\times2$ \emph{covariance}, and a
\emph{population fraction}. As a method check we refit on the
\emph{interface-excluded} bulk nodes only (dropping the high-$|\grad\phi|$
half, whose composition is not a bulk-phase value) and report the
endpoint shift as a \emph{sensitivity}.

\paragraph{What you should see.} The composition spread grows from
$\PfourSpreadStart$ (a tight blob) to $\PfourSpreadEnd$ as the blend
separates. The GMM finds two coexisting phases at $\PfourPhaseA$ (population
$\PfourPopA$) and $\PfourPhaseB$ (population $\PfourPopB$) --- one

\section{Admissibility, conservation, and the lever rule}

A trustworthy multi-component run must stay on the simplex, conserve each
solute, and respect the lever rule. The harness reports all three from the
shared \file{diffsim.diagnostics} library:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Simplex residual.} $\max|\phi_1+\phi_2+\phi_s-1| =
  \PfourSimplex$ --- machine zero, because the solvent is eliminated
  exactly ($\phi_s\equiv1-\phi_1-\phi_2$). Field bounds stay in-range
  ($\phi_1\in\PfourPhiOneRange$) with clipped fraction $\PfourClipFrac$
  (the log barrier keeps the fractions admissible).
\item \textbf{Quadrature conservation.} Integrating each solute by the
  \emph{assembly quadrature} (Gauss points $\times$ Jacobian, not a nodal
  mean), the per-solute content drifts are
  $|\Delta C_1|=\PfourDriftOne$ and $|\Delta C_2|=\PfourDriftTwo$ ---
  Model-B no-flux dynamics conserves each solute to round-off.
\item \textbf{Lever rule.} The conserved mean must equal the
  population-weighted average of the two phase compositions,
  $\bar{\vec\phi}=w_A\vec\phi^A+w_B\vec\phi^B$. The residual is
  $\PfourLever$ --- small, and a genuine cross-check that the clustering
  and the conservation agree.
\end{itemize}

\section{The \texorpdfstring{$N_i$}{N} shift}

Unequal degrees of polymerization move the spinodal. Because the entropic
Hessian terms in \eqref{eq:p4H} scale as $1/N_i$, a longer solute-1 chain
weakens the entropy that resists demixing and \emph{enlarges} the unstable
region. Running the asymmetric blend
$N=(\PfourAsymN,\PfourNtwo,\PfourNs)$ at the same composition, the critical
interaction drops from $\PfourChiCrit$ to $\PfourAsymChiCrit$ (a shift of
$\PfourDeltaChiCrit$) and $\det H$ deepens from $\PfourDet$ to
$\PfourAsymDet$. The stronger instability drives a more complete
separation --- the simulated phases move out to $\PfourAsymPhaseA$ and
$\PfourAsymPhaseB$, near the triangle edges
(Fig.~\ref{fig:p4nshift}).

\section{Code walk-through}

\paragraph{1. Materials.} \code{load\_blend("synthetic\_demix")}
(\file{materials/loader.py}) returns a validated $(\chi,N)$; the run never
hard-codes them.

\paragraph{2. The stepper.} \code{MultiPhaseStepper(dm, M=2, K=0,
chi\_aa=..., N=...)} builds the coupled $(\phi_1,\mu_1,\phi_2,\mu_2)$
system; \code{set\_initial([f1, f2])} seeds the two compositions.

\paragraph{3. Analysis (in \file{ternary.py}, not \file{src/}).}
\code{spinodal\_hessian} / \code{spinodal\_maps} give
\eqref{eq:p4H}; \code{gmm2} + \code{cluster\_phases} read off the phases
with the interface-excluded sensitivity; \code{predict\_binodal} solves
the common-tangent tie-line; the harness adds the quadrature conservation
and lever-rule residual.

\section{Explore on your own}

\question{Increase $\chi_{12}$ past $\chi_{12}^\star=\PfourChiCrit$ and
below it. Confirm from \code{spinodal\_hessian} that $\det H$ changes sign,
and that below $\chi_{12}^\star$ the noise decays (spread $\to0$) instead
of growing.}

\question{Make the solvent selective, $\chi_{1s}\neq\chi_{2s}$. Which
solute segregates preferentially, and how does the predicted binodal
\emph{tilt} on the triangle? Does the GMM sensitivity grow?}

\question{Push the asymmetry to $N_1=5$ or $10$. The spinodal enlarges
further --- but the quench also gets stiffer (weaker log barrier). Watch
the Newton iteration count and the reject ladder; this is why the tutorial
demonstrates $N_1=\PfourAsymN$ on a small mesh.}

\question{Turn off the off-diagonal mobility $M_{12}$. Does the tie-line
(the endpoints) change, or only the coarsening rate?}

\question{Map a real system from \file{materials/ternary\_p4.yaml} (say
\texttt{P3HT\_PCBM}) and predict, from its spinodal, whether the blend at a
chosen composition should demix. Then run it and check against the GMM
endpoints.}
