\chapter{Evaporation-induced crystallization}
\label{ch:p9}

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{Key idea.} This is the whole story, at tutorial scale: a
\emph{wet} film \emph{dries} (Ch.~\ref{ch:p5}), the concentrating blend
\emph{phase-separates} (Ch.~\ref{ch:p1}--\ref{ch:p4}), and once the
crystallizable species is concentrated enough its crystals
\emph{nucleate and grow} (Ch.~\ref{ch:p6}) coupled back to composition
(Ch.~\ref{ch:p7}). The arc \emph{wet film $\to$ phase separation $\to$
nucleation $\to$ crystalline film} is how a real solution-cast organic
solar cell forms.
\end{keybox}

\section{The model}

We run $(M,K)=(2,1)$ in film mode (Ch.~\ref{ch:p5}): a crystallizable
small molecule (species 0), a polymer (species 1), and an evaporating
solvent. The r14 crystallization bulk carries a crystal-contact penalty
$\chi_{ca}$, which gives a \emph{solubility}: crystallization is forbidden
below a local small-molecule fraction $\phi^\star$ set by the undercooling
versus $\chi_{ca}$. So the \emph{same} crystal seed behaves oppositely
depending on \emph{when} it is introduced:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item implanted in the \textbf{wet} film (solvent-rich, $\phi_0$ below
        $\phi^\star$) it \emph{dissolves};
  \item implanted \textbf{mid-drying} (drying has concentrated $\phi_0$
        above $\phi^\star$, and continuing solvent loss keeps deepening
        the quench) it \emph{grows} to a crystalline film.
\end{itemize}
That ordering --- crystallization strictly \emph{after} significant
solvent loss --- is the physics.

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{Seeding is physical and delicate} (the S3b campaign lessons). The
implanted embryo radius $r_0$ must clear the Gibbs--Thomson critical
radius $r^\star$ with margin (sub-critical embryos redissolve), and the
embryo amplitude must be near $1$ (a half-amplitude embryo halves the
bulk driving and doubles $r^\star$). We use $r_0$ with margin and
$\psi\approx0.95$, and read the \emph{terminal} crystalline state, not a
mid-growth snapshot.
\end{keybox}

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{Tutorial simplification} (recorded honestly). The production
configuration (\file{tests/test\_multiphase\_s3.py}) uses the Vignes
composition-singular mobility, whose 3-decade liquid$\to$solid drop is
faithful but stiff. Here we use a \emph{constant} Onsager mobility (with
the same cuDSS direct solver); the qualitative arc is unchanged, the
quantitative drying-front sharpness is softened.
\end{keybox}

\section{Run it}

\begin{runbox}
\textbf{Run it.}
\begin{lstlisting}
cd physics/09_evaporation_crystallization
python run.py            # wet-implant vs dry-implant
\end{lstlisting}
It dries the film and implants seeds either early (wet) or mid-drying,
and prints the drying state at implant and the terminal crystalline area
for each. Compare with \file{EXPECTED.md} and the figures.
\end{runbox}

\paragraph{What you should see.} Seeds implanted in the wet film
($\phi_s=\PnineWetPhis$) \emph{dissolve} (crystalline area
$\PnineWetStart\to\PnineWetEnd$, $\psi_{\max}=\PnineWetPsimax$). The same
seeds implanted mid-drying ($\phi_s=\PnineDryPhis$) \emph{grow} to a
crystalline film (area $\PnineDryStart\to\PnineDryEnd$,
$\psi_{\max}=\PnineDryPsimax$). The difference is entirely \emph{when}
they were introduced relative to the drying --- evaporation-induced
crystallization.

\section{Code walk-through}

\paragraph{1. The drying, crystallizing film.} \code{make\_stepper} builds
the $(2,1)$ film-mode stepper (r14, cuDSS) and asserts the crystal
energetics actually reached it --- the missing-splat guard from the S3b
campaign (a dropped keyword silently defaulted $T_m=1$ and turned the
driving into a huge \emph{melting} force).

\paragraph{2. Implant and march.} \code{run\_arc} marches the drying film
to the implant time, calls \code{implant} (a $\psi$ disc with margin over
$r^\star$ at the small-molecule-richest sites), and marches on to the
terminal, dried state.

\paragraph{3. Read the terminal state.} The fate is read from the
terminal crystalline area and $\psi_{\max}$, not a transient --- the
robust observables the S3b gates lock.

\section{Explore on your own}

\question{Sweep the implant time from wet to dry. Is there a sharp
crossover (a critical $\phi_s$) between dissolution and growth, and does
it match the r14 solubility $\phi^\star = 1 - \text{drive}/\chi_{ca}$?}

\question{Change the evaporation rate $k_e$ (Ch.~\ref{ch:p5}). Does faster
drying reach the crystallization window sooner, and does it change the
final crystalline morphology (grain size, coverage)?}

\question{Vary the implant radius $r_0$ around the critical radius. Find
the margin below which even a mid-drying seed redissolves --- the
Gibbs--Thomson $r^\star$ in the developing film.}

\question{Replace the hand-placed seed with FDT noise (Ch.~\ref{ch:p8}):
let the drying film nucleate on its own. Does crystallization still wait
for the solvent to leave? What sets the number of crystals?}

\question{This is the design problem: given a target (fine, pure,
bicontinuous crystalline domains), which knobs --- $k_e$, $\chi_{ca}$,
undercooling, blend ratio --- would you turn, and which fight each other?
(The Differentiable track answers this with gradients.)}
