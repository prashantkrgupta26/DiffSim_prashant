\chapter{Spatial and temporal adaptivity}
\label{ch:c3}
\begin{keybox}
\textbf{Key idea.} Resolution should follow the physics. In space,
\emph{octree refinement} puts small elements only where the field is
sharp (the interfaces) and leaves the bulk coarse. In time, an
\emph{LTE-controlled} step ladder shrinks $\Delta t$ during the violent
onset and grows it through slow coarsening --- but growing $\Delta t$
safely forces BDF2 to carry its \emph{variable-coefficient} form, or its
order silently collapses.
\end{keybox}

This concept measures both adaptivities on the Cahn--Hilliard brick. It
builds directly on the convergence study (Chapter~\ref{ch:c1}: the BDF2
order that adaptivity must preserve) and enables the long phase-field
horizons the physics track needs.

\section{Spatial adaptivity: octree refinement}

A spinodal field is sharp at its interfaces and smooth everywhere else.
A uniform mesh fine enough to resolve the interfaces wastes almost all
its elements on the flat bulk. An \emph{octree} mesh refines
adaptively: starting from one cell, it subdivides only where a criterion
says to. Here we refine any element within a band of a circular
interface and leave the rest coarse.

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{Hanging nodes.} When a fine element meets a coarse one, the fine
element has a node in the middle of the coarse element's edge --- a
\emph{hanging node} that is not a true degree of freedom. It is
\emph{constrained} to the average of its coarse neighbors, and that
constraint lives in the matrix $T$ (recall $A = T^{\!\top}KT$). The
solver never sees the hanging nodes as unknowns; \code{build\_constraints}
builds $T$, and the \emph{same} stepper runs unchanged --- which is why
the tutorial can just hand the CH brick an adaptive mesh and step.
\end{keybox}

The measured result: refining along one circle at level $\CthreeMaxLevel$
gives $\CthreeAdaptNodes$ nodes against $\CthreeUniformNodes$ for the
uniform mesh at the same finest $h$ --- a $\CthreeNodeSavings\times$
reduction in degrees of freedom (Fig.~\ref{fig:c3octree}), and the CH
brick steps on it. In 3-D the savings compound (a surface is a
vanishing fraction of a volume), which is what makes device-scale films
tractable at all (Chapter~\ref{ch:c5}).

\section{Temporal adaptivity: the LTE step ladder}

Phase separation is violent at onset and glacial during coarsening ---
the natural time scale spans orders of magnitude. A fixed $\Delta t$
must be small enough for the onset, wasting effort forever after. The
\emph{adaptive} march sizes each step to the physics using a
\emph{local truncation error} (LTE) estimate: it takes one step of size
$\Delta t$ and two of size $\Delta t/2$ from the same state, and the
difference (scaled by $2^{q}-1$) estimates the error of the step. If it
is below tolerance, accept and \emph{grow} $\Delta t$; otherwise reject,
halve, and retry.

The measured ladder over a quench to $t=0.8$: $\Delta t$ collapses to
its floor $\CthreeDtMin$ during onset, then climbs to $\CthreeDtMax$
during coarsening --- a range of $\CthreeSpan\times$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:c3ladder}). It reaches $t=0.8$ in $\CthreeNadaptive$
steps; a fixed march safe throughout would need the onset step size the
whole way --- about $\CthreeNfixed$ steps, a $\CthreeSavings\times$
saving.

\section{Why variable-coefficient BDF2}

Growing $\Delta t$ safely has a hidden cost. The textbook BDF2
coefficients $\tfrac32,\,[2,-\tfrac12]$ are derived assuming a
\emph{constant} step. Feed them a step that changed from the last one
and the scheme is only \emph{formally} second order --- its \emph{measured}
order collapses toward 1. The retrofit measured exactly this: on an
alternating $(\Delta t,\Delta t/2)$ sequence the constant-coefficient
form converged at order $\CthreeConstOrderLo/\CthreeConstOrderHi$
(audit doc, G3). The fix is to rebuild the coefficients each step from
the actual ratio $r=\Delta t/\Delta t_{\text{prev}}$:
%
\begin{equation}
  c_0 = \frac{1+2r}{1+r},\qquad
  \text{history weights } \big[\,1+r,\; -\tfrac{r^2}{1+r}\,\big],
\end{equation}
%
which reduces to the constant-step form at $r=1$ (bit-exactly, so
fixed-$\Delta t$ runs are unchanged) and stays consistent when $r\ne1$.
The tutorial re-measures it on the same alternating sequence: order
$\CthreeVarOrder$ --- second order restored.

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{This is why C1 and C3 are one story.} Adaptivity is worthless if
it silently destroys the accuracy you measured in Chapter~\ref{ch:c1}.
The LTE controller and the variable-coefficient scheme are a matched
pair: the controller \emph{varies} $\Delta t$; the variable-coefficient
BDF2 is what keeps the order-2 accuracy while it does.
\end{keybox}

\section{Code walk-through}

\paragraph{Octree from a refinement predicate.}
\begin{lstlisting}
def refine_fn(centers, h):        # True where to subdivide
    r = |centers - 0.5|
    return abs(r - 0.30) < 1.5*h  # a band around the circle
atree = build_adaptive(refine_fn, max_level=6, dim=2)
\end{lstlisting}
\code{build\_adaptive} subdivides until no flagged element can refine
further; \code{build\_constraints} then ties the hanging nodes.

\paragraph{The LTE-controlled march.}
\begin{lstlisting}
ts, dts = adaptive_march(st, t_end=0.8, tol=5e-4)   # returns the ladder
\end{lstlisting}
Inside, each step is step-doubled; accepted steps update $\Delta t$ by
$\text{safety}\cdot(\text{tol}/\text{LTE})^{1/(q+1)}$ (clamped). Rejects
rewind the state \emph{and} $\Delta t_{\text{prev}}$ --- so the BDF2
history spacing is never corrupted by a rejected step.

\paragraph{Measuring the variable-dt order.}
\begin{lstlisting}
ref = fixed_march(dt=2e-4)                    # fine reference
errs = [max|var_march((dt, dt/2)) - ref|  for dt in (8e-3,4e-3,2e-3)]
order = mean(log2(errs[i]/errs[i+1]))         # ~2.0, not ~1.0
\end{lstlisting}
The alternating $(\Delta t,\Delta t/2)$ sequence forces a fresh ratio
$r$ on every step --- the hardest test of the variable-coefficient form.

$10^{-4}$. How do the step count and the peak $\Delta t$ change? Verify
that the accuracy improves roughly in proportion to the tolerance (the
controller is doing its job).}

\question{Widen the octree refinement band from $1.5h$ to $3h$. How many
more nodes does it add, and does the CH step's cost track the node count
or the element count? Which is the right ``size'' of an adaptive mesh?}

\question{Refine along \emph{two} circles instead of one. Predict the
node count before running (a surface scales as its length here), then
check. How would this scaling differ in 3-D?}

\question{Force the constant-coefficient bug back: run the alternating
sequence but with a \emph{fixed} $\Delta t$ (so $r\equiv1$) and confirm
you recover order 2 --- then argue why the alternating sequence, not the
fixed one, is the test that exposes the variable-coefficient issue.}

\question{The onset drives $\Delta t$ to its floor. Lower the floor
\code{dt\_min} by $10\times$ and see whether the onset resolves better or
just wastes steps. What physically sets the smallest \emph{useful}
$\Delta t$ during spinodal onset?}
