\chapter{Substrate and boundary conditions}
\label{ch:p3}

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{Key idea.} A cast film is bounded by a \emph{substrate} and by
\emph{air}, and those interfaces are usually not neutral: one component
prefers the wall. That preference is imposed through the
\emph{boundary condition}, and it sets the \emph{vertical
stratification} of the morphology---which in a device decides whether
the right material meets the right electrode.
\end{keybox}

\section{The model}

The bulk equations are binary Cahn--Hilliard (Chapter~\ref{ch:p1}); the
new physics lives on the boundary. We keep the natural no-flux condition
on the sides and add a \emph{substrate wall free energy} on the bottom
edge $\Gamma_w$,
%
\begin{equation}
  F_w = \int_{\Gamma_w} f_w(\phi)\,dS, \qquad
  f_w(\phi) = g\,\phi + h\,\phi^2 ,
  \label{eq:fw}
\end{equation}
%
a surface energy density that is minimized at the preferred surface
composition $\phi^\star = -g/(2h)$. Taking the variation of the total
free energy adds a \emph{natural boundary term} to the chemical-potential
equation---the wall condition is
%
\begin{equation}
  \kappa\,\dd{\phi}{n}\Big|_{\Gamma_w} = -f_w'(\phi) = -(g + 2h\,\phi),
\end{equation}
%
so the $\mu$ residual gains $-\!\int_{\Gamma_w} N_a\,(g+2h\phi)\,dS$
(the consistent, basis-generic face term). Crucially this is a condition
on $\phi$, not on the \emph{mass} flux: the flux boundary condition
$\grad\mu\!\cdot\!n=0$ is unchanged, so \textbf{mass is still
conserved}---the wall changes \emph{where} material wants to be, not how
much there is.

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{Boundary-condition taxonomy.} A phase-field boundary can carry:
\emph{(i) natural / no-flux} ($\grad\mu\!\cdot\!n=\grad\phi\!\cdot\!n=0$):
neutral, nothing crosses, mass conserved---the default;
\emph{(ii) wall energy} (this chapter): a Neumann-type condition on
$\grad\phi$ from the surface energy $f_w'$; still no mass flux, but the
equilibrium is stratified;
\emph{(iii) Dirichlet}: pin $\phi$ or $\mu$ to a prescribed value (a
reservoir / fixed-composition contact)---this \emph{does} exchange mass
and is used mainly for manufactured-solution verification (Computational
track).
\end{keybox}

\section{Coefficients}

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{Wall coefficients and their meaning.} $g$ (linear surface field;
$g<0$ \emph{attracts} the component, $g>0$ repels it), $h>0$ (curvature
of the well, bounding the preference). Their ratio sets the preferred
surface composition $\phi^\star=-g/(2h)$, which must lie strictly inside
$(0,1)$ (a preference outside the physical range would drive $\phi$ into
the wall, where the Flory--Huggins logarithm is stiff). The tutorial
uses a weakly demixing blend ($\chi=\PthreeChi$) with $\phi^\star=0.75$
(attracting) and $\phi^\star=0.25$ (repelling) around a $\bar\phi=0.5$
blend. Real substrate energies are measured as contact-angle / surface-
energy asymmetries.
\end{keybox}

\end{lstlisting}

\end{runbox}

\paragraph{What you should see.} The neutral wall leaves the composition
unstratified (substrate $\approx\PthreeNeutralSub$, enrichment
$\PthreeNeutralEnr$). The attracting wall pulls the component to the
substrate (substrate $\approx\PthreeAttractSub$, enrichment
$\PthreeAttractEnr$); the repelling wall depletes it (substrate
$\approx\PthreeRepelSub$, enrichment $\PthreeRepelEnr$). In every case
the mass drift is $\le\PthreeMassdrift$: the wall re-arranges material
without creating or destroying it.

\section{Code walk-through}

\paragraph{1. Turn on the wall.} The wall energy is three constructor
arguments on the production brick:
\begin{lstlisting}
st = MultiPhaseStepper(dm, M=1, K=0, chi_aa=..., onsager=[[0.2]],
                       wall_g=[-1.5], wall_h=[1.0], wall_face=(1, 0))
\end{lstlisting}
\code{wall\_face=(1,0)} selects the $y=0$ (axis 1, min side) substrate
edge; \code{wall\_g}, \code{wall\_h} are $g,h$ of \eqref{eq:fw}. Setting
both to zero recovers the neutral no-flux boundary bit-for-bit.

\paragraph{2. Read the enrichment.} The tutorial builds a robust
node$\to$grid map (so ``substrate row'' is exactly the $y$-minimum) and
reports the lateral-mean $\phi$ per height---the enrichment profile of
Fig.~\ref{fig:p3profiles}.

\paragraph{3. Conditioning.} A low Onsager mobility ($0.2$) and the
\emph{bounded} quadratic well keep Newton at $\sim$4 iterations per step;
a preferred composition outside $(0,1)$, or a much larger mobility,
drives $\phi$ into the Flory--Huggins wall and collapses the step size
(a numerical point revisited in the Computational track).

\question{Move the preferred composition $\phi^\star$ toward $0$ or $1$
(increase $|g|$ at fixed $h$). At what point does the substrate layer hit
the Flory--Huggins wall, and how does the time step respond? Relate this
to the stiffness discussion in Chapter~\ref{ch:p2}.}

\question{Put the wall on the \emph{air} side (\code{wall\_face=(1,1)})
instead of the substrate, with the opposite preference. Does the
morphology invert? What does this imply for which component ends up at
the top vs bottom electrode?}

\question{Raise $\chi$ above the spinodal so the blend also demixes. Does
the attracting wall bias \emph{which} phase wets the substrate? Describe
the coupling between bulk phase separation and the surface preference.}

\question{Verify conservation explicitly: track the total mass over the
run for each boundary condition. Why does the wall energy, unlike a
Dirichlet condition, leave the mass exactly fixed?}

\question{Estimate the thickness of the enrichment boundary layer and
relate it to $\sqrt{\kappa}$ and to $\phi^\star-\bar\phi$. How would you
change $\kappa$ to make the substrate layer thinner?}
