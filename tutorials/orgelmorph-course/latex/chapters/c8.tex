\chapter{Extending DiffSim safely}
\label{ch:c8}
\begin{keybox}
\textbf{Key idea.} The bridge from \emph{using} a solver to \emph{contributing}
to one is a discipline, not a leap. Adding a new physics term is easy; adding
it \emph{safely} --- so the next person can trust it --- means carrying it
through a fixed sequence of gates: equations and units, a config knob, the
residual, the \emph{analytic} Jacobian, a finite-difference check, a
limiting-case check, a convergence study, the conservation and energy
invariants, a profile, and documentation. This chapter walks that entire
sequence on one small real term, and every gate is \emph{run} and passes.
\end{keybox}

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{Learning objectives.} By the end you can: add a new free-energy term to
a Cahn--Hilliard model with correct units; write its residual and its
\emph{analytic} tangent contribution; verify that tangent against a finite
difference; prove the extension reduces to the base model in the limiting case;
confirm the discretization still converges at the theoretical order; check that
mass and energy invariants still hold; profile the new term's cost; and write
the docs a reviewer needs. In short: turn a good idea into a contribution
someone else can build on.\\[2pt]
\textbf{Prerequisites.} The weak form $\to$ CUDA pipeline and the
red$\to$green Jacobian gate (Chapter~\ref{ch:c0}); convergence and MMS
(Chapter~\ref{ch:c1}); nonlinear diagnostics (Chapter~\ref{ch:c6}); profiling
(Chapter~\ref{ch:c7}); the Cahn--Hilliard energy (Physics
Chapter~\ref{ch:p1}).\\[2pt]
\textbf{Expected cost.} No GPU required --- the worked reference is a
transparent readable-NumPy solver on the diffsim mesh infrastructure (exactly
as Chapter~\ref{ch:c0}), so every stage is visible and cheap. \texttt{run.py}
and the \texttt{pytest} battery each finish in a few seconds on a laptop.
\end{keybox}

This chapter extends Chapter~\ref{ch:c0}'s hands-on (add a Jacobian term to a
scalar problem) to a \emph{full} contribution on a conserved, mixed system. The
worked reference lives in \file{sextic\_ch.py} (the solver) and
\file{test\_sextic\_ch.py} (its gate); \file{run.py} walks the whole workflow
and prints a \texttt{PASS/FAIL} for each stage (Fig.~\ref{fig:c8workflow}).

\section{Step 1 --- the term, its equations, and its units}

The base Cahn--Hilliard bulk energy is the quartic double well
$f_0(c)=\tfrac14(c^2-1)^2$. We add a sixth-order Landau term --- a higher-order
stabilizer that steepens the well and bounds deep quenches:
%
\begin{equation}
  f(c) = \tfrac14(c^2-1)^2 + \frac{\beta}{6}\,c^6 ,\qquad
  f'(c) = c^3 - c + \beta\,c^5 ,\qquad
  f''(c) = 3c^2 - 1 + 5\beta\,c^4 .
\end{equation}
%
The new coefficient $\beta$ is dimensionless (energy-scaled), and
$\beta=0$ recovers the base model \emph{exactly} --- the limiting case we will
test. Only two derived quantities matter downstream: $f'(c)$, which enters the
chemical-potential residual, and $f''(c)$, which enters the Jacobian. Getting
the analytic $f''$ contribution $5\beta c^4$ right is the crux of the
contribution.

\section{Step 2 --- a config knob}

A new physical parameter is a new config field, validated and defaulted through
the course schema (Chapter~00's harness), never a hard-coded constant:
\begin{lstlisting}
"beta": Field(float, default=0.5, min=0.0),   # sixth-order Landau coefficient
\end{lstlisting}
The resolved value is written to \file{config.resolved.yaml}, so every run
records exactly which $\beta$ produced it.

\section{Steps 3--4 --- residual and \emph{analytic} Jacobian}

The mixed backward-Euler weak form is $R_c=0,\;R_\mu=0$ with
%
\begin{align}
  R_c   &= \intv w\,\frac{c-c^{\,n}}{\Delta t}\dV
         + M\!\intv \grad w\cdot\grad\mu\dV, \\
  R_\mu &= \intv q\,\mu\dV - \intv q\,f'(c)\dV
         - \kappa\!\intv \grad q\cdot\grad c\dV .
\end{align}
%
The new term rides in through $f'(c)$: the only nonlinear assembly,
$\int N_a f'(c)$, gains $\beta c^5$. Newton then needs the tangent
$J=\partial R/\partial x$, whose four blocks per node are
%
\begin{equation}
  J_{cc}=\frac{M_e}{\Delta t},\quad
  J_{c\mu}=M\,K_e,\quad
  J_{\mu c}=-M_e^{f''}-\kappa K_e,\quad
  J_{\mu\mu}=M_e ,
\end{equation}
%
with $M_e$ the mass and $K_e$ the stiffness element matrices and
$M_e^{f''}=\intv N_a\,f''(c)\,N_b\dV$ the $f''$-weighted mass matrix. The new
term enters exactly one block, $J_{\mu c}$, through
$f''(c)=3c^2-1+5\beta c^4$. This is the single most error-prone line in any
contribution --- so we never trust it by eye.

\section{Steps 5--6 --- verify the derivative, and the limiting case}

\paragraph{Finite-difference check.} The analytic tangent must match a finite
difference of the residual, $J\,v\approx[R(x+\varepsilon v)-R(x)]/\varepsilon$.
On a random admissible state the relative error is \CeightJacFd\ (and
\CeightJacFdBase\ at $\beta=0$) --- limited only by finite-difference
truncation, the signature of a \emph{correct} tangent (a wrong $f''$ would sit
at $O(1)$, exactly Chapter~\ref{ch:c0}'s red$\to$green contrast). The new term's
own derivatives check too: $f_6'=\beta c^5$ and $f_6''=5\beta c^4$ agree with a
finite difference to \CeightTermFp\ and \CeightTermFpp.

\paragraph{Limiting case.} A safe extension must \emph{not} change the base
model where it shouldn't. At $\beta=0$ the new-term value, first, and second
derivatives vanish identically and $f',f''$ reduce to $c^3-c$ and $3c^2-1$ --- so
the extended solver reproduces the base double well bit-for-bit. This guards
against the classic regression: a ``harmless'' addition that quietly perturbs
existing results.

\section{Step 7 --- convergence still holds}

Adding a term must not break the discretization's order of accuracy. Using a
steady manufactured solution with the term active (Chapter~\ref{ch:c1}'s method:
manufacture $c^\ast,\mu^\ast$, add the residual they leave as sources, pin them
on the boundary), the $L^2$ error of $c$ falls from \CeightMmsCoarse\ to
\CeightMmsFine\ over three mesh levels at observed order \CeightMmsOrder\ ---
the theoretical $p{+}1=2$ for linear elements (Fig.~\ref{fig:c8verify}, left).
The manufactured source carries the new $\beta c^5$ term, so this is a genuine
test of the extended residual, not the base one.

\section{Step 8 --- conservation and energy still hold}

The two physical invariants of Cahn--Hilliard must survive the change. A
no-flux march conserves the quadrature mass $\int c\dV$ to \CeightMassDrift\
(relative) --- Model-B transport moves material, never creates it. And the
Ginzburg--Landau energy $F=\int[f(c)+\tfrac{\kappa}{2}|\grad c|^2]\dV$ decreases
monotonically, \CeightEnergyStart\ $\to$ \CeightEnergyEnd, with largest positive
step increment \CeightEnergyInc\ --- the discrete Lyapunov property intact
(Fig.~\ref{fig:c8verify}, middle). A term that broke either invariant would be
physically wrong no matter how well it converged.

\section{Step 9 --- profile the cost}

A contribution should know its own cost (Chapter~\ref{ch:c7}). Timed with
CUDA-synchronized stage timers, the new term adds only a fifth power evaluation
to the residual and a fourth power to the Jacobian --- its assembly overhead is
negligible, within timing noise of the base assembly. The term buys a real
physical effect (a steeper well, bounded deep quenches;
Fig.~\ref{fig:c8verify}, right) for essentially no cost.

\section{Step 10 --- documentation and changelog}

The last gate is the one that makes the work \emph{reusable}: docstrings stating
the equations and units, the schema field, an \file{EXPECTED.md} of the passing
gates, and a \file{CHANGELOG.md} entry naming the term, its parameter, and the
tests that cover it. An undocumented correct term is a landmine for the next
contributor.

\section{Code walk-through}

\paragraph{The new term, isolated.} \file{sextic\_ch.py} keeps the term as three
one-liners so the derivative test targets it directly:
\begin{lstlisting}
def sextic_term(c, beta):
    return dict(f=(beta/6)*c**6, fp=beta*c**5, fpp=5*beta*c**4)
\end{lstlisting}

\paragraph{Where it enters.} The residual gains $\beta c^5$ in one place
(\texttt{fprime}), the Jacobian gains $5\beta c^4$ in one block
(\texttt{fdoubleprime} inside $J_{\mu c}$). Every other line is the base model
--- the smallest possible diff is the safest.

\section{Explore on your own}

\question{Add a \emph{different} term --- a cubic tilt $\tfrac{\gamma}{3}c^3$
(which breaks the well's symmetry). Write $f',f''$, add them, and run the full
gate. Which invariant is most sensitive to getting the tilt's Jacobian wrong?}

\question{Deliberately break the tangent: drop the $5\beta c^4$ from
\texttt{fdoubleprime}. Which gates fail, and which still pass? Explain why the
MMS study can still look plausible while the FD check screams.}

\question{Make $\beta$ a \emph{field} $\beta(\mathbf x)$ (spatially varying).
What changes in the residual, the Jacobian, and the units? Re-run the FD check.}

\question{Push $\beta$ to $2$ and re-quench from a large-amplitude state. Does
the steeper well keep $|c|$ more tightly bounded than the base model? Relate the
bound to the balance $c^3\sim\beta c^5$.}

\question{Port the term to the production Warp kernel
(\file{cahn\_hilliard.py}: \texttt{make\_ch\_newton}). What must you add for the
device path that the transparent NumPy reference did not need, and how would you
re-run this exact gate against the device implementation?}
