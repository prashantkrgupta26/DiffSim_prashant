\chapter{Validation and uncertainty}
\label{ch:c10}
\begin{keybox}
\textbf{Key idea.} ``Is the answer right?'' is not one question but six:
code verification, solution verification, model validation, parameter
uncertainty, stochastic variability, and model--experiment discrepancy.
Conflating them is the most common way a computational study misleads ---
a converged run can still be swamped because a material input is known
only to order of magnitude. This chapter separates the six and closes
with the deliverable that forces the separation: an \emph{uncertainty
budget} that names the dominant source of error.
\end{keybox}

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{Learning objectives.} By the end you can name and separately
measure all six error questions; propagate a material parameter
uncertainty from \file{materials.yaml} to a morphology observable by a
finite-difference sensitivity; combine parametric, stochastic and
numerical standard deviations in quadrature into one budget; and read off
the dominant source --- and therefore where the next effort should go (a
better $\chi$, more seeds, or a finer mesh).\\[2pt]
\textbf{Prerequisites.} Computational Chapter~\ref{ch:c1} (code and
solution verification --- the MMS order study cited here),
Chapter~\ref{ch:c9} (the seed ensemble and the $\chi$ sweep whose
sensitivity is reused), the \file{materials.yaml} schema and loader (the
parameter-uncertainty source), and the stochastic diagnostics.\\[2pt]
\textbf{Expected cost.} Any CUDA GPU, 2-D, $<1$\,GB device memory. The
budget campaign is $\approx13$ real runs (a \CtenNseeds-seed ensemble, a
six-run sensitivity, a three-level mesh refinement, a linear-stability
probe); $\approx2$--$3$\,min on an RTX~6000 Ada.
\end{keybox}

\section{Six questions, not one}

A number from this simulator can be wrong in six independent ways, and a
defensible study answers each separately:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Code verification} --- am I solving the equations
        \emph{right}? Order/MMS, conservation, energy monotonicity: a bug
        check against \emph{mathematics}.
  \item \textbf{Solution verification} --- is the \emph{discretization}
        error of \emph{this} run small enough? Mesh refinement of the
        observable.
  \item \textbf{Model validation} --- is it the \emph{right model of
        reality}? Comparison against an independent benchmark.
  \item \textbf{Parameter uncertainty} --- how much does the observable
        move because a material input has a \emph{spread}?
  \item \textbf{Stochastic variability} --- how much does it move
        \emph{seed to seed} at fixed parameters?
  \item \textbf{Model--experiment discrepancy} --- the residual gap once
        the rest are accounted.
\end{enumerate}
The trap is to answer one and imply the others: ``it converged, so it is
right'' confuses~(2) with~(1) and~(3); ``mean $\pm$ seed-sd'' reports~(5)
alone and hides~(4). We use one material case and one observable to keep
all six commensurate.

\section{One material case, one observable}

The case is \CtenMaterial, read from \file{materials.yaml}: the Flory
interaction $\chi_{\mathrm{pf}}=\CtenChiNom$ with a reported spread
$\CtenChiSigma$ (order of magnitude --- the OSC literature has no single
``true'' $\chi$ for this blend). We take that spread as the $1\sigma$
parametric uncertainty on $\chi$. The parameter and its uncertainty are
pulled through the validated loader, never hand-copied. $\chi$ enters the
binary Cahn--Hilliard free energy as the enthalpic $B$
(Chapter~\ref{ch:c9}'s engine); the observable is the interfacial-area
domain length $L_{\mathrm{area}}$, with nominal value $\CtenLnominal$.

\section{Verification: code, then solution}

\paragraph{Code verification (1).} Two invariants a real bug would break:
Model-B dynamics conserve mass (measured drift $\CtenMassDrift$) and the
free energy is a Lyapunov functional (monotone decrease, largest positive
increment $\CtenLargestInc$). The full spatial/temporal order verification
is Chapter~\ref{ch:c1}'s manufactured-solution study --- cited, not
repeated.

\paragraph{Solution verification (2).} Refining the mesh from
$\CtenSideCoarse^2$ to $\CtenSideFine^2$ moves $L_{\mathrm{area}}$ from
$\CtenLcoarse$ through $\CtenLmid$ to $\CtenLfine$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:c10verif}, left). The change over the last refinement,
$\CtenDiscUnc$, is our \emph{numerical} uncertainty on the observable ---
the discretization contribution to the budget.

\section{Model validation (3)}

Verification asks whether we solved the equations right; validation asks
whether they are the right equations. Absent lab data we validate against
an \emph{independent physical benchmark}: Cahn--Hilliard linear stability
predicts the fastest-growing \emph{onset} wavelength
$\lambda^\ast=2\pi/k^\ast$. A tiny-$\Delta t$ growth-spectrum probe
measures the early-time fastest mode; its peak lands at
$\lambda_{\mathrm{meas}}=\CtenLamMeas$ against the predicted
$\lambda^\ast=\CtenLamStar$, a $\CtenValidPct\%$ discrepancy. Note the
subtlety: the budgeted observable $L_{\mathrm{area}}$ is the
\emph{coarsened} end state, many times $\lambda^\ast$ --- so the
\emph{onset} probe, not the end field, is the correct benchmark
(comparing the coarsened length to $\lambda^\ast$ gives a spurious
two-hundred-percent ``discrepancy''). Experimental validation would need
measured $S(q)$; that is the honest gap in facet~(6).

\section{Uncertainty: parametric and stochastic}

\paragraph{Parameter uncertainty (4).} A central finite-difference
sensitivity (each side averaged over seeds to strip the noise) gives
$\mathrm{d}L_{\mathrm{area}}/\mathrm{d}\chi\approx\CtenDLdchi$. Times the
material's $1\sigma$ spread $\CtenChiSigma$ this is a parametric standard
deviation $\sigma_{\mathrm{param}}=\CtenSigmaParam$.

\paragraph{Stochastic variability (5).} A \CtenNseeds-seed ensemble at
fixed $\chi$ gives a seed-to-seed standard deviation
$\sigma_{\mathrm{stoch}}=\CtenSigmaStoch$ --- the same kind of ensemble
spread Chapter~\ref{ch:c9} aggregated.

\section{The uncertainty budget}

Independent sources add in \emph{variance}, so we combine them in
quadrature (Fig.~\ref{fig:c10budget}):
%
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_{\mathrm{total}}
  = \sqrt{\sigma_{\mathrm{param}}^2 + \sigma_{\mathrm{stoch}}^2
          + \sigma_{\mathrm{num}}^2}
  = \CtenSigmaTotal,
\end{equation}
%
with $\sigma_{\mathrm{num}}=\CtenSigmaNum$ the discretization term. The
result is unambiguous: the \textbf{\CtenDominant\ uncertainty dominates},
$\CtenParamPct\%$ of the variance --- $\CtenDomRatio\times$ the next
largest --- against $\CtenStochPct\%$ stochastic and $\CtenNumPct\%$
numerical. The combined uncertainty is $\CtenRelTotal\%$ of the observable.
Because $\chi$ is known only to order of magnitude, the morphology's
uncertainty is set by the \emph{material input}, not the seed noise or the
mesh. The actionable conclusion: to sharpen this prediction, measure
$\chi$ better --- do \emph{not} buy more GPU seeds or refine the mesh.

\section{Code walk-through}

The six facets and the budget live in \file{uncertainty.py}.

\paragraph{1. Read the material and its uncertainty.}
\begin{lstlisting}
mat  = load_system("P3HT_PCBM")            # materials.yaml, validated
chi0 = mat.param("chi_polymer_fullerene").value        # 0.86
sig  = mat.param("chi_polymer_fullerene").uncertainty  # order_of_mag 0.5
\end{lstlisting}

\paragraph{2. Propagate: sensitivity times spread.}
\begin{lstlisting}
dLdchi     = (L(chi0+d) - L(chi0-d)) / (2 d)   # noise-averaged over seeds
sigma_param = abs(dLdchi) * sig                # facet 4
sigma_stoch = sd(L over seeds at chi0)          # facet 5
\end{lstlisting}

\paragraph{3. Combine in quadrature, name the dominant source.}
\begin{lstlisting}
sigma_total = sqrt(param^2 + stoch^2 + num^2)
dominant    = argmax(variance contributions)    # -> parameter (chi)
\end{lstlisting}

\section{Explore on your own}

\question{Build the budget for a \emph{different} material in
\file{materials.yaml} (e.g.\ PDPP5T:PCBM). Does the parametric source still
dominate? For which materials would the seed noise win, and why?}

\question{Reproduce the onset-vs-coarsened trap: compare the end-state
$L_{\mathrm{area}}$ wavelength to $\lambda^\ast$ and watch the ``discrepancy''
balloon past $200\%$. Explain why the early-time probe is the only fair
model-validation benchmark here.}

\question{Add a fourth budget term: the temporal (time-step) uncertainty,
by refining $\Delta t$ the way facet~2 refines the mesh. Where does it rank?}

\question{Interpret ``order of magnitude'' as a factor (log-normal) rather
than an additive $\pm0.5$. Recompute $\sigma_{\mathrm{param}}$. Does the
\emph{dominance} survive the reinterpretation?}

\question{The combined $\sigma$ is $\CtenSigmaTotal$, not the arithmetic sum
$\CtenSigmaParam+\CtenSigmaStoch+\CtenSigmaNum$. Explain why independent
sources add in variance, and by how much the arithmetic sum would overstate
the true uncertainty.}
