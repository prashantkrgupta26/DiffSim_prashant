\chapter{Nonlinear solver diagnostics}
\label{ch:c6}
\begin{keybox}
\textbf{Key idea.} Every implicit phase-field step is a \emph{nonlinear}
solve. Whether a run is trustworthy, merely slow, or silent garbage is
decided inside the Newton loop --- and that loop leaves a readable trail:
residual and update norms, safeguard activity, a condition number. This
chapter teaches you to read that trail, to recognize the handful of ways a
nonlinear solve fails, and to act on each. The failures here are not
hypothetical: we \emph{run} them and they genuinely fail.
\end{keybox}

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{Learning objectives.} By the end you can: read a Newton solve's
residual $\Vert r\Vert$ and update $\Vert\delta x\Vert_\infty$ per iteration
and recognize quadratic convergence; choose between an absolute, relative, and
residual stopping test and say when each misleads; explain what the trust
clamp and the box projection do and see them act; and diagnose the four
canonical failures --- Newton stagnation, a min-$\Delta t$ wall, an
ill-conditioned Jacobian, and a diverging start --- from their signatures,
using the decision tree, and leaving a failure checkpoint behind.\\[2pt]
\textbf{Prerequisites.} Newton's method and its quadratic convergence; the
mixed Cahn--Hilliard weak form and its analytic Jacobian
(Chapter~\ref{ch:c0}); adaptive time stepping and local truncation error
(Chapter~\ref{ch:c3}); Physics Chapter~\ref{ch:p1} (the brick solved here).
\\[2pt]
\textbf{Expected cost.} Any CUDA GPU, 2-D, $<0.5$\,GB device memory; solver
\texttt{splu} (exact to round-off, so Newton is the only nonlinear knob).
\texttt{run.py} runs every diagnostic in $\approx$20--30\,s on an RTX~6000 Ada
(mostly Warp kernel compile + Python start-up); the studies are single- and
few-step.
\end{keybox}

This chapter instruments the \emph{production} Cahn--Hilliard Newton solve
(\file{src/diffsim/physics/cahn\_hilliard.py}, the brick of
Chapters~\ref{ch:p1} and~\ref{ch:c1}) into a repeatable diagnostic workflow.
We do not modify the stepper. The per-iteration trajectory it does not log we
reconstruct through its public API --- re-solving the same step capped at
$1,2,3,\dots$ Newton iterations and reading its \texttt{last\_newton} record
and captured system --- which is exact because a Newton solve from a fixed
state is deterministic.

\section{Anatomy of a healthy Newton solve}

Each Cahn--Hilliard step solves the coupled nonlinear system
$R(x)=0$ for $x=(c,\mu)$ by Newton's method: assemble the residual $R$ and the
tangent $J=\partial R/\partial x$, solve $J\,\delta x=-r$ (with $r=R$), update
$x\leftarrow x+\delta x$, repeat. The single most informative thing you can
watch is the pair of norms per iteration: the residual $\Vert r\Vert$ (how far
the equations are from satisfied) and the update $\Vert\delta x\Vert_\infty$
(how much the solution still moves). With a \emph{correct analytic Jacobian}
Newton is \textbf{quadratically} convergent: near the root the residual is
roughly squared each step. On a gentle poly quench we measure

2 & $2.0\times10^{-8}$ & $1.2\times10^{-6}$ & residual squared \\
3 & \CsixHealthyResEnd & $1.6\times10^{-15}$ & at round-off \\
\bottomrule

Three iterations, the residual falling from \CsixHealthyResZero\ to
\CsixHealthyResEnd\ (Fig.~\ref{fig:c6newton}, green). That squaring is the
fingerprint of a correct tangent; if you see \emph{linear} convergence instead,
suspect the Jacobian (Chapter~\ref{ch:c0} shows how a missing Jacobian term
degrades exactly this curve). This is also the honest way to separate
\emph{algebraic} error (an unconverged Newton solve) from \emph{discretization}
error (Chapter~\ref{ch:c1}): only once $\Vert r\Vert$ is at tolerance is the
step's error purely the discretization's.

\section{When have you converged? Three stopping tests}

``Converged'' is a choice, not a fact. Three tests are in common use, all at
the same tolerance $\tau$:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{residual} $\Vert r\Vert<\tau$: the equations are nearly
        satisfied --- the most physical test, but its scale is the residual's,
        not the solution's;
  \item \textbf{absolute update} $\Vert\delta x\Vert_\infty<\tau$: the iterate
        has stopped moving --- what the stepper actually uses;
  \item \textbf{relative update} $\Vert\delta x\Vert_\infty/\Vert x\Vert<\tau$:
        the iterate has stopped moving \emph{relative to its own size}.
\end{itemize}
On this well-scaled problem, where $c$ and $\mu$ are $O(1)$, all three fire at
the same iteration~\CsixHealthyIters. They part company the moment the field
is rescaled: multiply the solution by $10^{-3}$ (a change of units, not of
physics) and the \emph{absolute} update test now trips $10^3\times$ sooner ---
it is blind to scale. The lesson: an absolute update tolerance is a statement
about your units; prefer a residual or a relative test, or verify that your
fields are $O(1)$ before trusting an absolute one.

\section{Safeguards: what keeps a hard step alive}

A raw Newton step off a bad iterate can be enormous or unphysical. The
production stepper carries two safeguards, and a diagnostic run should confirm
they are working rather than assume it.

\paragraph{Trust clamp.} A composition increment beyond $2$ units is always a
diverging transient (poly's physical range is $[-1,1]$, Flory--Huggins' is
$(0,1)$). When $\max|\delta c|>2$ the stepper rescales the \emph{whole} update
to preserve its direction while capping its length. Unclamped, a quench-onset
step at large $\Delta t$ drives $|c|\sim37$, where $f''=3c^2-1\approx4100$
poisons any linear solve.

\paragraph{Box projection (Flory--Huggins).} The FH bulk energy
$f(c)=A[c\ln c+(1-c)\ln(1-c)]+Bc(1-c)$ has $f''(c)\sim A/c$ near the wall
$c\to0$; the kernel regularizes the logarithm with a cap $1/\varepsilon=10^4$.
To keep early Newton iterates off that cap, each iterate is clipped into
$[10^{-3},1-10^{-3}]$, and the stepper records how many dofs it moved and by
how much. On a deep FH quench ($B=8$) the projection moves \CsixSafeProjDofs\
dofs (largest correction \CsixSafeProjMax) and the step still converges ---
exactly the intended behaviour: the safeguard absorbs the overshoot instead of
letting it detonate the solve.

\section{Four ways a nonlinear solve fails}

An honest workflow rehearses its failures. Each case below is \emph{run} and
genuinely fails; the skill is reading the signature and taking the right
action (Fig.~\ref{fig:c6decision}).

\paragraph{1. Newton stagnation (the step size is too large).} Take a deep poly
quench --- a high-amplitude random field --- at $\Delta t=\CsixStagDt$. The raw
Newton direction diverges, so the trust clamp fires on \emph{every} iteration
(clamp fraction \CsixStagClampFrac); the residual crawls down only linearly and
after the stepper's full budget of \CsixStagIters\ iterations is still
\CsixStagResFinal, many orders from tolerance (Fig.~\ref{fig:c6newton}, red).
The trap is that it \emph{looks} like slow progress. The fix is not more
iterations --- it is a smaller step: the identical quench at
$\Delta t=\CsixStagFixedDt$ converges in \CsixStagFixedIters\ iterations. A
persistently clamped update with sub-quadratic residual decay means ``reduce
$\Delta t$,'' full stop.

\paragraph{2. The min-$\Delta t$ wall.} An adaptive integrator shrinks
$\Delta t$ until its local-truncation-error (LTE) estimate meets a tolerance
(Chapter~\ref{ch:c3}). Ask for an LTE tolerance it cannot reach and set the
floor $\Delta t_{\min}$ too high, and the controller is trapped: every step is
a forced accept at the floor. We drive \CsixMindtSteps\ steps, all at
$\Delta t_{\min}$, and measure the LTE \emph{at the floor} directly ---
$\CsixMindtLte$, far above the requested tolerance \CsixMindtTol. The
signature (constant $\Delta t=\Delta t_{\min}$ with LTE $>$ tol) says the
controller has run out of room: loosen the LTE tolerance, or accept ---
knowingly --- the floor's accuracy. Silently ``succeeding'' at the floor is the
failure to avoid.

\paragraph{3. An ill-conditioned Jacobian.} As a Flory--Huggins state
approaches the wall, $f''(c)\sim A/c$ climbs toward the $1/\varepsilon$ cap and
the tangent's condition number climbs with it. Freezing a uniform state at each
mean composition and forming its Jacobian, the exact 2-norm condition number
rises from \CsixCondBulk\ at $c=0.5$ to \CsixCondWall\ at $c=0.003$ ---
$\CsixCondRatio\times$ worse (Fig.~\ref{fig:c6conditioning}). An ill-conditioned
tangent starves an iterative linear solver and erodes accuracy in a direct one;
the cure is upstream --- keep iterates off the wall (the projection above), or
precondition. This is why the projection is not cosmetic.

\paragraph{4. A diverging start (wrong Jacobian or bad initial guess).} If
convergence is linear from the very first step, or the residual grows, suspect
the tangent itself or an inadmissible initial condition. Verify the Jacobian
against a finite difference (Chapter~\ref{ch:c0}'s red$\to$green gate) and seed
a physical initial state. This case is diagnosed, not with a number, but by
ruling the others out.

\section{Never drop a failed step: checkpoints}

A production campaign must never silently discard a step that failed to
converge --- that is how a bad result masquerades as a good one. When the
stagnation case fails, the run writes a \textbf{failure checkpoint} to
\file{checkpoints/stagnation\_step.npz}: the initial and last Newton states,
the full residual/update history, the convergence flag, and the exact config
to reproduce it. A post-mortem then starts from data, not from a re-run that
may not reproduce.

\section{Code walk-through}

The diagnostics live in \file{diagnostics\_ch.py}; they drive the production
stepper unchanged.

\paragraph{1. Reconstruct the per-iteration trajectory.}
\begin{lstlisting}
for k in range(1, kmax+1):                  # cap Newton at k iterations
    st = CahnHilliardStepper(..., newton_max=k, capture_system=True)
    st.set_initial(ic, mu_init="consistent"); st.step()
    A, r = st.last_system                    # the k-th assembled system
    res.append(norm(r)); dx.append(st.last_newton["dx_inf"])
\end{lstlisting}
Deterministic, so the $k$-th entry is exactly the $k$-th internal iterate --- no
stepper edits needed.

\paragraph{2. Detect the safeguards.} A clamped iteration is
$|\,\Vert\delta x\Vert_\infty-2\,|<10^{-9}$; the projection exposes
\texttt{st.proj\_dofs} and \texttt{st.proj\_max} directly.

\paragraph{3. Measure the min-$\Delta t$ LTE.} One $\Delta t_{\min}$ step versus
two $\Delta t_{\min}/2$ steps from the same state gives the step-doubling LTE
(Chapter~\ref{ch:c3}); at the floor it exceeds the requested tolerance.

\section{Explore on your own}

\question{Reproduce the stagnation case, then bisect $\Delta t$ between
\CsixStagDt\ and \CsixStagFixedDt\ to find the largest step that still
converges within the \CsixStagIters-iteration budget. How does that critical
$\Delta t$ scale if you halve the mesh size $h$?}

\question{The absolute update test tripped $10^3\times$ early when the field
was rescaled by $10^{-3}$. Implement a relative-residual test
$\Vert r\Vert/\Vert r_0\Vert<\tau$ and show it is invariant to that rescaling.
Which test would you make the default, and why?}

\question{Push the Flory--Huggins conditioning sweep to $c=10^{-4}$ (closer to
the wall). Does cond$(J)$ keep climbing, or does the $1/\varepsilon$ cap flatten
it? Relate the plateau to the regularization constant $\varepsilon=10^{-4}$.}

\question{Turn the stagnation failure into a \emph{line search}: after the
clamp, backtrack the step ($\delta x\leftarrow\alpha\,\delta x$, halving
$\alpha$ until the residual decreases). Does the deep quench now converge at
$\Delta t=\CsixStagDt$? What does that cost in iterations versus simply
reducing $\Delta t$?}

\question{Open the failure checkpoint \file{checkpoints/stagnation\_step.npz}
and plot \texttt{c\_last}. Where on the domain is the unconverged residual
largest --- and does that location coincide with the sharpest composition
gradient?}
