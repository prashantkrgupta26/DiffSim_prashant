\chapter{Evaporation: the drying film}
\label{ch:p5}

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{Key idea.} A film is cast from solution and \emph{dries}: solvent
leaves the top surface, the film thins, and the solutes concentrate until
they demix. Evaporation is the \emph{clock} of morphology formation. But
the honest question is subtle: does the drying \emph{rate} reshape the
morphology, or does it merely decide \emph{when} you look? We compare
morphologies at a \emph{matched dryness} (the same mean solvent fraction
$\phi_s$, not the same time) and find that the \emph{degree} of demixing
is set by dryness alone --- essentially rate-independent --- while the
domain \emph{size} collapses onto a single Biot number
$\mathrm{Bi}=k_e h_0/D_s$.
\end{keybox}

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{Learning objectives.} By the end you can: write the moving-frame
film equations ($K=\sum_i k_{e,i}\overline{\phi_i^{\text{top}}}$,
$\dd{h}{t}=-K$) atop the ternary bulk dynamics of
Chapter~\ref{ch:p4}; explain why a matched-\emph{time} rate comparison
confounds rate with dryness and design the matched-$\phi_s$ fix; define
$\mathrm{Bi}=k_e h_0/D_s$ and \emph{verify} that different $(k_e,D_s)$
sharing a $\mathrm{Bi}$ collapse onto one morphology; keep apart
``dryness sets the demixing degree'' from ``$\mathrm{Bi}$ sets the domain
size''; and report per-solute conservation and the height budget as a
resolution-limited invariant.\\[2pt]
\textbf{Prerequisites.} The ternary free energy, spinodal, and mobility
machinery of Chapter~\ref{ch:p4} (reused verbatim here); the interface
width/resolution intuition of Chapter~\ref{ch:p1}; Chapter~00
(environment green).\\[2pt]
\textbf{Expected cost.} Any CUDA GPU, 2-D (level 5--7). Solver: cuDSS,
with \code{splu} a documented fallback for small runs. Quick mode
target $<2$\,min (comparable to Chapter~\ref{ch:p1}'s measured
$\approx75$\,s); reference (7-run sweep) 5--30\,min; research widens to a
finer $(k_e,D_s)$ regime grid.
\end{keybox}

\section{The model}

We add a moving, evaporating top surface to the ternary system of
Chapter~\ref{ch:p4} (the Landau-mapped moving frame of the
Wodo--Ganapathysubramanian film formulation). The computational box
$[0,1]^2$ represents a physical strip of shrinking height $h(t)$; the
vertical coordinate maps as physical $z = h(t)\,\xi_y$. The solvent flux
at the top drives the frame,
%
\begin{equation}
  K = \sum_i k_{e,i}\,\overline{\phi_i^{\text{top}}} \;\ge 0,
  \qquad \dd{h}{t} = -K ,
  \label{eq:p5film}
\end{equation}
%
and each conserved solute gains a frame-advection term plus a surface-
enrichment flux, arranged so the solute \emph{content} $h\!\int\phi_i$ is
conserved exactly per BDF1 step (only the solvent leaves). The bulk
dynamics is the ternary Cahn--Hilliard of Chapter~\ref{ch:p4}.

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{The Biot number $\mathrm{Bi}=k_e h_0/D_s$.} $k_e$ is the
nondimensional evaporation rate of the solvent; $D_s$ is the solute
Onsager mobility (its diffusivity scale). Their ratio is the
\emph{drying Biot number} --- equivalently the drying P\'eclet number in
this unit-aspect geometry --- comparing how fast the surface recedes with
how fast the solutes diffuse. $\mathrm{Bi}>1$ is \emph{drying-limited}
(the film skins before it can homogenise); $\mathrm{Bi}<1$ is
\emph{diffusion-limited}. Because $\mathrm{Bi}$ mixes $k_e$ and $D_s$,
different solvents/temperatures ($k_e$) and different blends ($D_s$) that
share a $\mathrm{Bi}$ dry to the \emph{same} morphology --- we test that
collapse directly.
\end{keybox}

\section{Matched terminal state: rate versus dryness}

A naive sweep compares each rate at the end of a fixed time horizon. That
\emph{confounds} two different things: a slow film may only have reached
$\phi_s=0.30$ while a fast one has dried to $\phi_s=0.10$, so any
morphology difference could be entirely the different \emph{dryness}, not
the rate. The fix is to drive \emph{every} rate past the same dryness and
read each morphology metric at a \emph{matched} $\phi_s\in\{0.30,0.20,
0.10\}$, interpolated along the run's own $\phi_s(t)$ trajectory
(Fig.~\ref{fig:p5matched}). What is confounded in the naive comparison,
and de-confounded here, is exactly this rate-versus-state distinction.

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{The morphology metric is a real length.} We take the first
spectral moment of the \emph{lateral} (periodic-$x$) structure factor of
the polymer field --- a mean cycles-per-box $\bar k$ --- and report the
wavelength both as a fraction of the box width, $\lambda/L_x=1/\bar k$,
\emph{and} in cells, $\lambda/\Delta x = n_x/\bar k$. A resolved length is
always $>1$ cell; the earlier code reported a bare ``cells'' number below
one, which conflated the box-width fraction with a cell count. We report
$\ge 2$ metrics: the lateral wavelength, the interfacial length, and the
phase contrast.
\end{keybox}

\paragraph{What you should see.} Every film dries and thins
(Fig.~\ref{fig:p5drying}); the march reports \emph{why} it stopped, and we
never call a horizon-truncated run ``dried''. At $k_e=\PfiveKeFast$
($\mathrm{Bi}=\PfiveBiFast$) the film reaches the dryness cutoff
(\texttt{\PfiveReasonFast}) by $t\approx\PfiveTdryFast$; at
$k_e=\PfiveKeSlow$ ($\mathrm{Bi}=\PfiveBiSlow$) it takes until
$t\approx\PfiveTdrySlow$ (\texttt{\PfiveReasonSlow}). Reading morphology at
\emph{matched} dryness (Fig.~\ref{fig:p5matched}) tells the real story:

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Dryness sets the \emph{degree} of demixing.} The phase
        contrast rises monotonically as the film dries
        ($\PfiveContrastThirty\to\PfiveContrastTwenty\to\PfiveContrastTen$
        at $\phi_s=0.30,0.20,0.10$) --- and does so \emph{identically for
        every rate}: at a matched $\phi_s=0.20$ the contrast agrees across
        all runs to $\PfiveContrastSpread\%$. How far the blend has
        separated is fixed by how \emph{dry} it is, not by how fast it got
        there.
  \item \textbf{The domain \emph{size} collapses onto $\mathrm{Bi}$.} At
        matched dryness the lateral wavelength depends on the Biot number
        ($\lambda$ at $\phi_s=0.20$: $\PfiveWLslowTwenty$ cells for slow
        ($\mathrm{Bi}=\PfiveBiSlow$), $\PfiveWLfastTwenty$ for fast
        ($\mathrm{Bi}=\PfiveBiFast$)), and runs that \emph{share} a
        $\mathrm{Bi}$ from different $(k_e,D_s)$ agree to
        $\PfiveCollapseSpread\%$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:p5regime}). With only
        $\sim$2 lateral domains across the box the wavelength is
        statistically noisy, so we report the collapse, not a sharp
        size-versus-$\mathrm{Bi}$ law.
  \item \textbf{The old ``faster $=$ finer'' was the confound.} It compared
        a \emph{dried} fast film against a \emph{still-wet} slow one --- a
        dryness difference misread as a rate effect. De-confounded, the
        robust, rate-independent quantity is the demixing \emph{degree}
        (contrast), and the size is governed by $\mathrm{Bi}$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Per-solute conservation and the height budget}

Only the solvent evaporates, so each solute's content $h(t)\!\int\phi_i$
must be constant. We integrate $\int\phi_i$ by quadrature on the fixed
reference domain (the P1 lumped-mass weights) and multiply by $h(t)$; the
relative drift over the whole run is $\PfiveBalPoly$ (polymer) and
$\PfiveBalFull$ (fullerene) --- at solver tolerance, confirming the
moving-frame scheme conserves solute. The height budget then closes
identically: since the solutes are conserved, the solvent lost equals the
height drop, $h_0-h(t)$, to $\PfiveBudgetClosure$. Over the slow run the
film loses $\PfiveSolvRemoved\%$ of its solvent and thins by
$\Delta h=\PfiveDhSlow$.

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{Conservation is resolution-limited, and we say so.} This exact
per-step conservation holds \emph{once the demixed interfaces and the
surface enrichment layer are resolved}. On a too-coarse mesh (level 4,
$16\times16$) the same run leaks $\sim10\%$ of a solute; at level 5--6 the
drift falls to machine precision ($\sim10^{-15}$). The tutorial's
\texttt{quick} tier therefore uses level 5, the coarsest mesh that still
conserves --- a concrete lesson that a conservation law in the continuous
model is only inherited by the discretisation you can afford to resolve.
\end{keybox}

\section{The Biot regime map}

Sweeping \emph{both} $k_e$ and the solute mobility $D_s$ places each run in
the $(k_e,D_s)$ plane (Fig.~\ref{fig:p5regime}), with
$\mathrm{Bi}=k_e h_0/D_s$ iso-contours. Two observations:
%
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Morphology collapses onto $\mathrm{Bi}$.} Runs that share a
        Biot number --- reached by different combinations of a faster
        solvent (larger $k_e$) or a slower blend (smaller $D_s$) --- land on
        the same matched-dryness wavelength, to $\PfiveCollapseSpread\%$.
        The pair $(k_e,D_s)$ does not matter; only their ratio does.
  \item \textbf{The crossover is $\mathrm{Bi}=1$.} Our sweep spans
        $\mathrm{Bi}\in[\PfiveBiMin,\PfiveBiMax]$, straddling the
        drying-limited/diffusion-limited boundary. This is the map a
        process engineer reads: pick a solvent and a blend, compute
        $\mathrm{Bi}$, and you know which regime you are casting in.
\end{itemize}

\section{Code walk-through}

\paragraph{1. Film mode.} A single constructor argument turns on the
moving frame:
\begin{lstlisting}
st = MultiPhaseStepper(dm, M=2, K=0, ...,
                       film=dict(k_e=0.3, h0=1.0))
\end{lstlisting}
\code{k\_e} is the solvent evaporation rate, \code{h0} the initial
height. The stepper carries \code{h\_curr}, updated $-\Delta t\,K$ on each
accepted step.

\paragraph{2. The drying march.} \code{march(..., phis\_stop=0.08,
h\_min=0.12)} adds film stop criteria and returns the \emph{exit reason}
(\texttt{phis\_stop}, \texttt{h\_min}, \texttt{dt\_underflow},
\texttt{t\_end}); the harness records it per run.

\paragraph{3. The matched-state readout.} \code{run\_film} records the
morphology metrics along the whole $\phi_s(t)$ trajectory;
\code{morphology\_metrics} returns the lateral wavelength (fraction
\emph{and} cells), the interfacial length, and the contrast. The harness
interpolates each to the matched $\phi_s$ and computes
$\mathrm{Bi}=k_e h_0/D_s$.

\question{Widen the Biot range (push $D_s$ down and $k_e$ up). Does the
matched-dryness wavelength ever break its collapse onto $\mathrm{Bi}$, or
is $\mathrm{Bi}$ the only group that matters here?}

\question{Change the solvent selectivity ($\chi_{1s}$ vs $\chi_{2s}$).
Does a solvent that prefers one solute change which component enriches at
the drying surface --- and does that show up more in the \emph{vertical}
(physical-frame) profile than in the lateral wavelength?}

\question{The drying curve $\phi_s(t)$ is roughly linear early
(constant-rate period) then bends. Explain the bend from the surface
composition feeding back into $K=\sum_i k_{e,i}\overline{\phi_i^{\text{top}}}$.}

\question{Combine this with Chapter~\ref{ch:p3}: add a substrate wall
energy and dry the film. Does evaporation-driven enrichment at the top
compete with substrate enrichment at the bottom? Which wins, and at what
$\mathrm{Bi}$?}

\question{Re-run at level 4 and watch the per-solute drift blow up. At
what mesh does conservation recover? Relate the answer to the interface
width $\ell\sim\sqrt{\kappa/W}$ of Chapter~\ref{ch:p1}.}
