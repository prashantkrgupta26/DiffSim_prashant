\chapter{Coupled Cahn--Hilliard $+$ Allen--Cahn}
\label{ch:p7}

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{Key idea.} The real morphology problem is \emph{coupled}:
composition (Cahn--Hilliard) and crystallinity (Allen--Cahn) evolve
together and feed back. The signature of the coupling is
\emph{crystallization-driven demixing} --- as a species crystallizes it
enriches its own phase and expels the others, sharpening a composition
pattern that phase separation alone would not produce.
\end{keybox}

\section{The model: the four-fold $\chi$ (p1 vs r14)}

With crystallinity, the interaction between two species depends on whether
each is amorphous or crystalline, so the single Flory $\chi$ becomes
\emph{four} matrices $\chi_{aa},\chi_{ac},\chi_{ca},\chi_{cc}$ (with
$\chi_{ca}=\chi_{ac}^{\!\top}$). \textbf{The convention differs between the
two bulk free energies}, and getting this right is the crux of the chapter.

This chapter runs the \textbf{p1} bulk, whose $\chi_{\text{eff}}$ is the
\emph{mutually-weighted} combination of all four terms:
%
\begin{equation}
  \chi_{\text{eff}}^{\text{p1}}(i,j) =
    (1-\psi_i)(1-\psi_j)\,\chi_{aa} + (1-\psi_i)\psi_j\,\chi_{ac}
  + \psi_i(1-\psi_j)\,\chi_{ca} + \psi_i\psi_j\,\chi_{cc}.
\end{equation}
%
At the four \emph{pure} limits this collapses to a single matrix each, so
in p1 the four $\chi$ are \textbf{absolute} pair interactions:
$\chi_{\text{eff}}(0,0)=\chi_{aa}$, $(1,0)=\chi_{ca}$, $(0,1)=\chi_{ac}$,
$(1,1)=\chi_{cc}$ (measured here $\ensuremath{\PsevenChiAA}$,
$\ensuremath{\PsevenChiCA}$, $\ensuremath{\PsevenChiAC}$,
$\ensuremath{\PsevenChiCC}$). By contrast the \textbf{r14} bulk uses
$\psi^2$-weighted \emph{increments} on top of $\chi_{aa}$,
%
\begin{equation}
  \chi_{\text{eff}}^{\text{r14}}(i,j) = \chi_{aa}
   + \psi_i^2\,\chi_{ca} + \psi_j^2\,\chi_{ac}
   + \psi_i^2\psi_j^2\,\chi_{cc},
\end{equation}
%
so there the same crystalline-amorphous input $\chi_{ca}$ means an
\emph{increment} $\chi_{aa}+\chi_{ca}$ at the pure limit (e.g.
$\ensuremath{\PsevenChiCAincr}$), \emph{not} an absolute value.
\file{test\_chi\_limits.py} verifies all four pure limits for both
conventions against the production evaluator.

Making the crystal-contact interaction \emph{larger} than $\chi_{aa}$
means ``a crystal of species $i$ dislikes amorphous species $j$ more than
two amorphous phases dislike each other'' --- crystallizing $i$ pushes $j$
out. Together with the crystal-bulk energy (which enriches $i$ where it
crystallizes) this is the thermodynamic origin of crystallization-driven
demixing.

\section{Measuring the coupling: commensurate controls}

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{No divide-by-floor.} We do \emph{not} compare a crystal run against
a differently-masked no-crystal run (that amplified a
near-zero denominator). We run \textbf{three commensurate controls} ---
\code{ch\_only} ($K{=}0$), \code{coupled\_nochi} (crystal present but
$\chi_{ca}{=}\chi_{aa}$), and \code{full} ($\chi_{ca}{>}\chi_{aa}$) --- and
score all three with the \emph{same} mask (the full run's $\psi>0.5$
footprint) and the \emph{same} metric (the species-0 composition contrast
$\langle\phi_0\rangle_{\text{in}}-\langle\phi_0\rangle_{\text{out}}$). We
report \emph{absolute} contrasts and the attributable increments; a
relative amplification is quoted \emph{only} when the denominator is
resolved.
\end{keybox}

The controls decompose the demixing into two physical channels:
%
\begin{align}
  \text{crystal-bulk channel} &=
     \text{coupled\_nochi} - \text{ch\_only}
     = \ensuremath{\PsevenCrystChannel}, \\
  \text{$\chi$-expulsion channel} &=
     \text{full} - \text{coupled\_nochi}
     = \ensuremath{\PsevenChiChannel}.
\end{align}
%
On the crystal footprint ordinary Cahn--Hilliard produces essentially no
contrast (\code{ch\_only} $=\ensuremath{\PsevenCtrlChonly}$). \emph{Most}
of the demixing is the \textbf{crystal-bulk} channel --- the
$\phi_k^2\,W(\psi_k)$ term enriches the crystallizing species where it
orders, present even with $\chi_{ca}{=}\chi_{aa}$. The \textbf{$\chi$
expulsion} adds a further, smaller increment (full
$=\ensuremath{\PsevenCtrlFull}$; relative to \code{coupled\_nochi} that is
$\ensuremath{\PsevenRelFullNochi}\times$, denominator resolved). The
contrast is reproducible across seeds (Sec.~\ref{sec:p7run}).

\section{Coupled energy budget}

The coupled film is a gradient flow of a single free energy
$F=F_{\text{entropy}}+F_{\chi}+F_{\text{cryst}}+F_{\text{grad}\,\phi}
+F_{\text{grad}\,\psi}$, evaluated by quadrature with the exact production
densities (\code{coupled\_energy}). Over the quench the total decreases
monotonically ($F:\ensuremath{\PsevenEnergyStart}\to
\ensuremath{\PsevenEnergyEnd}$, largest positive step
$\ensuremath{\PsevenEnergyMaxPos}$) --- the coupling redistributes energy
between the mixing, $\chi$ and crystal terms but cannot increase the total
(Fig.~\ref{fig:p7budget}, left).

\section{Causality}

Correlation is not cause; we perturb the coupling to check its direction.
\textbf{Frozen geometry:} freeze the crystal ($L_\psi{=}0$) and run the
composition with vs without the $\chi$ coupling --- the coupling still adds
demixing at \emph{fixed} geometry (increment
$\ensuremath{\PsevenFrozenChi}$), so the coupling, not the \emph{act} of
crystallizing, drives it. \textbf{Kinetics at fixed coupling:} a fast
($L_\psi{=}6$) and a slow ($L_\psi{=}1$) run both reach a large contrast
($\ensuremath{\PsevenKinFast}$ vs $\ensuremath{\PsevenKinSlow}$) --- the
\emph{coupling} sets the demixing magnitude while the kinetic rate sets
only the timing.

\paragraph{What you should see.} On the shared crystal-footprint mask the
composition contrast is $\ensuremath{\PsevenCtrlChonly}$ (CH only),
$\ensuremath{\PsevenCtrlNochi}$ (crystal-bulk channel) and
$\ensuremath{\PsevenCtrlFull}$ (full) --- so crystallization-driven
demixing is dominated by the crystal-bulk channel with a smaller
$\chi$-expulsion increment. The total free energy decreases monotonically,
and the causality experiments confirm the coupling (not the kinetics)
causes the demixing.

\section{Code walk-through}

\paragraph{1. Four $\chi$ matrices + the convention test.} \code{\_chis}
builds $\chi_{aa}$ (all pairs) and the crystal-contact $\chi_{ca}$;
\code{chi\_eff\_limits} and \file{test\_chi\_limits.py} pin the p1
(absolute) vs r14 (increment) pure limits against the production evaluator
\code{\_np\_chi\_eff}.

\paragraph{2. Commensurate controls.} The \texttt{run} entry point takes \texttt{mode} one of
\texttt{ch\_only}, \texttt{coupled\_nochi}, or \texttt{full}; the
\texttt{contrast\_on\_mask} routine scores every control on the one
shared mask.

\paragraph{3. Energy + causality.} \code{coupled\_energy} integrates the
exact free-energy split; \code{freeze\_psi=True} ($L_\psi{=}0$) gives the
frozen-geometry control, and varying \code{L\_psi} gives the kinetics
control.
