\section*{What this chapter will teach}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The scaling verdict.} The cuDSS direct solver \emph{cannot}
  factorize the film hero (15.15M DOF, 1.6B nnz) on a GH200 --- an
  HBM-saturation capacity wall, identical in fp64 and fp32. So the
  100M-DOF pathway is \textbf{iterative, not direct}, which is exactly why
  the projection engine (SPD-PPE $\to$ AMG/AMGX) is the load-bearing choice.
\item \textbf{The AMGX PPE study (task W1).} The Leray stepper's SPD PPE
  driven through AMGX (PCG $+$ classical-AMG V-cycle) on 3-D uniform meshes:
  per-solve time, AMG iteration count, residual, memory, and the
  single-GPU DOF ceiling. Textbook AMG scaling; 100M fits in roughly
  33\,GB/GPU. Parity check vs \code{splu} at small levels.
\item \textbf{Device $K_p$ assembly (the mesh-build wall).} The A100/GH200
  forensics found a ${\sim}61$\,s/step host-side floor (assembly / scatter /
  GP fields) common to all three GPU generations --- a silent Amdahl
  ceiling. The chapter measures it, then shows the device-assembled $K_p$
  path that removes it, and the \emph{one-time} mesh-build cost that
  remains.
\item \textbf{The L8$\to$GH200 path.} The PPE scalar DOF map for a 3-D
  uniform octree at level $L$ is $n=(2^L+1)^3$: L7 ${\sim}$2.1M,
  L8 ${\sim}$16.6M, L9 ${\sim}$133M. 100M sits between L8 and L9. The
  study reveals where the single-GPU ceiling actually bites (the Warp
  $2^{31}$ array-shape limit vs host COO$\to$CSR assembly) and the
  deployment tier (single big node: GH200/NVL4) it targets.
\end{itemize}
